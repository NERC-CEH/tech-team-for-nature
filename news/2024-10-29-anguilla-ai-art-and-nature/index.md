Anguilla's passion for nature and innovation came together on Tuesday 29 October 2024, as community members gathered at Good Korma on the Strip for an evening that blended science, creativity and cutting-edge technology. The event, led by Tom and Jenny from the UK Centre for Ecology & Hydrology, drew an enthusiastic crowd eager to explore how artificial intelligence and biodiversity data are reshaping the way we understand, and even see, our natural world.

From the outset the atmosphere was warm and buzzing. Attendees settled in with light refreshments as the presenters introduced the ways AI can transform raw ecological data into vivid, meaningful art. These visualisations help scientists interpret complex patterns in nature, and invite the public to appreciate biodiversity in new and unexpected ways.

One of the evening's highlights was the unveiling of a new collaborative project between the Anguilla National Trust and UKCEH. It uses deep learning to identify and monitor insects, one of the planet's largest and most essential groups of animals, and promises more efficient biodiversity monitoring and insights that could strengthen conservation across the island.

The presentation was free and open to the public, and the spirit of openness carried through the evening, with thoughtful questions, shared observations and lively discussion with the visiting scientists.

This work is supported by the UK Government's Biodiversity Challenge Funds through Darwin Local, which continues to help bring expertise and new tools to Anguilla's conservation.

As the night wrapped up, one thing was clear: "this wasn't just a presentation, it was a glimpse into the future of how we study, protect, and celebrate the natural world."
